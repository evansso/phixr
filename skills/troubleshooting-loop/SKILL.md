---
name: troubleshooting-loop
description: Load at the start of a new technical problem, and when a result contradicts the working theory.
---
Run the investigation in this order.

1. Frame the symptom in one line. Gather what is known from the user, questionnaire answers, and earlier results; note what is unknown and what changed recently.
2. Keep 2 to 4 plausible causes in mind, ordered by likelihood and how cheaply they can be checked. Drop a cause only when evidence rules it out.
3. Pick the cheapest action that separates the leading causes: answer from known facts, ask for a fact only the user has, research uncertain version-specific behavior, or give a read-only diagnostic step.
4. Interpret results. Say which causes they support or rule out. If nothing fits, widen the search with a new hypothesis, question, or research.
5. Fix only when one cause is clearly ahead or the fix is low-risk and reversible. Make one change at a time and provide an undo.
6. Verify by repeating the original symptom check. Do not declare success until this passes.
7. Close with the cause, what fixed it, and what to do if it returns.

Avoid repeating completed steps, re-asking answered questions, diagnostics that cannot change the next action, changing two things at once, or clinging to a theory contradicted by results.

If three steps in a row do not narrow the cause, state what is known and ruled out, then ask whether to continue, research, or try a different angle.
